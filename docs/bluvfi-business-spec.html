The bigger idea

Bluvfi Business started as an app: an AI CFO for one company's treasury. The bigger version is a network.

Bluvfi Business is the verifiable trust layer for agent payments. Before any AI agent moves money, Bluvfi Business checks the counterparty, enforces the human-set policy in a deterministic gate backed by a custodian the agent cannot override, signs the AI's judgment in attested hardware, and leaves evidence an auditor can verify. Every company that uses it makes the fraud network smarter for every other agent.

Card networks became valuable partly because of their fraud networks: every transaction makes detection better for everyone. The agent economy has no equivalent. Bluvfi Business has two sides that feed each other:

  1. Bluvfi Business Treasury, the app: an AI finance agent for Trezu, NEAR Business-style treasuries or any NEAR treasury. It pays invoices, collects receivables and buys services, and every move is verifiable.
  2. Bluvfi Business Check, the network: the counterparty check and shared scam registry, sold per check to any agent over x402 and Agent Market.

The answer to Haseeb, built in: "Agents run fine on cards, for buying things. But a card can't tell you whether the agent was tricked, whether the payee is a scammer, or prove to your auditor what happened. Bluvfi Business works on every rail: Intents for treasury payments, x402 for APIs, cards later."

Layer 1: Policy, set by humans and enforced without a contract

A deterministic policy gate in the app, backed by a treasury the agent cannot spend from, enforces every rule below. Policy changes only through a multisig, and every version is hashed. Layer 1 is complete as far as it can go without a contract, and it has been tested for real: against a live Sputnik DAO on NEAR testnet (real council votes, the chain refusing the agent's key), and through the web app on 3 November. Custody options (set with CUSTODY)

  • sputnik: the DAO holds everything; the agent files Payment Requests in Trezu's own format, and the council's vote executes them. Votes can be gasless through the relayer.
  • outlayer: an OutLayer policy wallet; its limits, allowlist and approvers are enforced in Intel TDX, and the agent holds only an API key.
  • hybrid (new): the DAO is the vault and an OutLayer wallet is a small spending wallet. Payments under the approval threshold are paid from the wallet; larger ones, or ones the wallet cannot cover, go to the council. OutLayer must cap each payment at the threshold or the app refuses to pay. When the wallet runs low, the agent files one top-up proposal for the council. The wallet's balance is the most that can ever leave without a council vote.
  • manual: a signer sends from their own wallet and records the transaction.

What only a contract of our own could add: an onchain hold window, and onchain limits and allowlist for payouts that go through the DAO. With hybrid custody the gap is small: routine payments are capped outside the app, and anything larger needs a council vote. Settled funds on other chains can never be frozen, and the app says so plainly.

Built in o1js, in TypeScript: (1) a payee address is not on the OFAC list and (2) a vendor name is not on it (exact normalised match, covering every listed name and alias), both without saying who: the list becomes a Merkle tree of gaps whose public root anyone can recompute from OFAC's list, and a listed item cannot be proved clear; (3) a vendor or business passed identity verification and (4) the treasury held at least $X, each attested by k of n independent issuers (this app plus up to two attesters run by someone else, which check the fact themselves: a balance is read off the NEAR chain by each, and none will sign a figure above what it sees; a verification is confirmed with Persona, for the right business and vendor), and one key listed twice counts once; (5) a secret circuit of up to 2,048 gates uses at most Q qubits and T Toffoli gates, proved a chunk of 32 gates at a time by recursion, without showing a gate. The set of issuer keys and the threshold can be put to the treasury DAO and, once the council approves it, is recorded on the NEAR chain, so a verifier can check the keys a proof names are the approved ones (no contract of ours). A proof is a file that anyone verifies on a public page, /verify-proof (no account: paste or choose the file, and it says in plain words what is proved, who the issuers are and what it rests on, with optional requirements such as an issuer you trust and how many must have signed), with npm run zk:claim:verify, or through the public /api/claims/verify. Run for real: 19 tests with real proofs (forgeries, one key passed off as two, expired and stale inputs, swapped circuits and changed statements all fail); on real data (the whole real OFAC address list and all 38,712 listed names and aliases as roots, a real mainnet USDC balance read by two issuers, a 100-gate circuit over four recursive chunks); and the issuer anchor on the real testnet DAO with real council votes. Honest limits that remain: attestation reduces trust but does not remove it, because proving a balance with no issuer at all needs a proof of NEAR's chain state (a light-client proof, which is zkVM work in Rust); the circuit claim bounds a committed gate list of up to 2,048 gates and does not prove the numbers are the true cost of a full-scale circuit or that it computes an attack; a name one letter off a listed one is not covered by the proof (ordinary screening still warns on it); building the name tree takes about two minutes the first time for each list date; and Kimchi proofs are not post-quantum (a hash-based STARK would be needed).

Submission

Tracks: realworld, security, business, privacy, agents.

Description draft:

AI agents are starting to pay invoices and buy services, but nobody can verify what an agent was told, who it's paying, or whether it was tricked. Bluvfi Business is a verifiable trust layer for agent payments on NEAR. Human-set spending policy is enforced by a deterministic gate and a custodian the agent cannot override: a Sputnik DAO run in Trezu, an OutLayer policy wallet, or both, with no contract of ours. The agent holds only a key that can propose a payment, checked from the chain before every payout.
